About Me

I'm Rohit Jain, a Cloud, DevOps, and Platform Engineer with nearly 8 years of experience and expertise in building highly available, fault-tolerant, secure, and cost-effective AWS platforms. I take cloud initiatives from architecture and design through automation, deployment, and ongoing operations, balancing reliability, security, and cost at every stage. At Experity, I worked as part of the teams supporting platforms serving more than 5,700 clinics and 28 million annual patient visits. My work connects cloud architecture, security, and automation with the reliability engineering teams need to deliver healthcare technology.

I bring hands-on technical leadership to infrastructure and delivery initiatives: designing repeatable AWS provisioning and CI/CD workflows, standardizing engineering environments, and coordinating changes and incident response across Development, Security, and IT. I led a one-click AWX/Ansible rollout of CrowdStrike and Rapid7 to more than 200 servers, reducing manual errors, and led the design and deployment of serverless API-usage reporting that automated monthly Finance reports with 99% accuracy at under $15 per month.

I helped the organization achieve millions of dollars in cloud savings and cost avoidance through resource tagging, identifying and optimizing underutilized resources, and building serverless architectures. I also removed 47 TB of excess storage, saving $900 per month ($10,800 annually) while preserving data-retention requirements. I also standardized secure testing environments for EMR and AI Scribe, validating performance with up to 200 concurrent users across 15 to 36 clinics.

My technical foundation spans Kubernetes, Docker, CloudFormation, Python/Boto3, Bash, PowerShell, IAM, networking, and CI/CD. I work with least-privilege access, MFA, cross-account trust, CloudWatch monitoring, CloudTrail audit logging, and controlled changes. Alongside enterprise platform work, I independently deploy generative AI and RAG applications using Amazon Bedrock, Claude, Nova, Knowledge Bases, and Guardrails.
